A registered business can open a business bank account, apply for loans and government programmes, supply bigger companies and bid for contracts. Many support programmes ask for a registration certificate before anything else.

## Which type of registration?

- **Sole proprietorship (business name):** one owner, simplest and cheapest. Most micro businesses start here.
- **Partnership:** two or more people running the business together.
- **Company limited by shares:** a separate legal body from its owners. More paperwork, but it can protect your personal property and is often expected by bigger customers and investors.

Registration is done with the **Office of the Registrar of Companies (ORC)**. Check the ORC website (orc.gov.gh) or visit an ORC office for the current forms and fees, as they change from time to time.

## Registering a business name: the usual steps

1. **Choose a name** and check it is not already taken.
2. **Have your Ghana Card ready.** Your Ghana Card number is also used as your tax number (TIN) as an individual.
3. **Fill in the registration form** with the business name, what the business does, the owner's details and the business address (your GhanaPost digital address).
4. **Pay the fee** and submit.
5. **Collect your certificate.** Keep it safe and make copies.

## Don't forget to renew

Business names must be **renewed every year**. A late renewal can cause problems when you apply for a loan or contract, so set a reminder.

## Permits to check

Registration is not the only paper you may need. Depending on your trade:

- **Business operating permit** from your Municipal or District Assembly, usually renewed yearly.
- **Food and drugs:** products such as packaged food, drinks, cosmetics and herbal products usually need **Food and Drugs Authority (FDA)** registration before you sell them in shops.
- **Standards:** some products need to meet **Ghana Standards Authority (GSA)** standards.
- **Health certificate** for food handlers, from the local health office.

Ask your District Assembly, the FDA, or a **GEA Business Advisory Centre** which ones apply to you.

## Need help?

GEA Business Advisory Centres in many districts help small businesses with registration and paperwork.
