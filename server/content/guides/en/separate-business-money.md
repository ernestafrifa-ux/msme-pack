When business money and house money share one pocket, it is hard to tell if the business is really making a profit. Money for school fees, a funeral contribution or a family visit quietly leaves the business, and at the end of the month you cannot say where it went.

Separating the two is the single most useful money habit for a small business.

## 1. Give the business its own place for money

Choose one place where only business money goes:

- a cash box or envelope kept only for the business, or
- a separate MoMo wallet (a second SIM works well), or
- a bank account in the business name.

Customers pay into it. Business costs are paid from it. Nothing else.

## 2. Pay yourself a fixed amount

Decide what you will take for yourself each week or month, and take only that. In this app, record it as **owner pay**, not as an expense.

Start small. If the business does well for a few months, you can raise it.

## 3. Don't borrow from the box

If you take business money for something personal, write it down and pay it back. Small "borrowings" add up quickly and are the main reason businesses run out of money to restock.

## 4. Build a cushion for slow months

Every week, move a small amount into a separate savings place, even GHS 20. Aim for enough to cover one month of rent, wages and restocking. This is what keeps the business alive when sales drop in the rainy season, or a big customer pays late.

## Quick check

- [ ] The business has its own cash box, MoMo wallet or account
- [ ] I pay myself a set amount and record it
- [ ] I write down and repay anything I borrow from the business
- [ ] I save a little every week for slow months
