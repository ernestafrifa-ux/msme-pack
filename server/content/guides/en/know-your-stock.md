Stock is money sitting on your shelf. Too little and customers walk to the next shop. Too much and your cash is stuck, or goods expire.

## Step 1: Put your products in the app

Add each product with its cost price, selling price and how many you have now. From then on:

- every **sale** with products takes them out of stock,
- every **Buy stock** adds them,
- **sent invoices** take out what you sold on credit.

## Step 2: Set a warning level

For each product, ask: *how many do I sell in the time it takes to restock?*

**Example:** you sell about 5 jars a day, and it takes 4 days to cook a new batch. Set the warning level at **20 jars** (5 × 4). When you reach 20, the app shows **Running low** on the Home screen, and you know it's time to cook or buy.

## Step 3: Count regularly

Records drift: something gets broken, a child takes a sweet, a sale is not recorded. Count your stock:

- **weekly** for fast-moving or valuable items,
- **monthly** for everything else.

Use **Do a stock count**. Type what you actually find, and the app records only the differences.

## Step 4: Look at the differences

If the count keeps coming up short for the same item, find out why:

- Are sales being missed?
- Is it getting damaged or expiring? Record it as damaged or expired so you can see how much it costs you.
- Is someone taking it?

## Step 5: Sell first what came first

Put new stock behind old stock, so older items are sold first. This matters most for food, drinks and cosmetics with expiry dates.
