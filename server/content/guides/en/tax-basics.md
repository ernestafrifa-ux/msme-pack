Paying tax is part of running a formal business, and being tax compliant opens doors: government contracts, some loans and support programmes ask for it.

The **Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA)** runs a simpler system for small, informal businesses called the **Modified Taxation Scheme (MTS)**.

> Tax rules change. Treat this guide as a starting point and confirm what applies to you with GRA, at a Taxpayer Service Centre or through your trade association.

## Your tax number

As an individual, your **Ghana Card number** is your Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). Have it ready when you register for anything with GRA.

## The Modified Taxation Scheme

It is for individuals in the informal sector, such as traders, artisans and vendors. GRA groups businesses by their yearly sales:

- **Sales of GHS 20,000 a year or less (on average):** you pay a **fixed amount** set by GRA for your type and size of business.
- **Sales above GHS 20,000 and up to GHS 500,000 a year:** you pay **3% of your total sales**.
- **Others** (for example professionals, people with several businesses or outlets, partners in a registered partnership, or anyone who chooses it) are taxed on **profit**: sales minus allowable business costs.

This is why keeping sales records matters: your records show which group you belong in and how much you owe.

## How to register and pay

- **Register** with your Ghana Card, GhanaPost digital address and phone number, using GRA's **MTS Taxpayer App**, at a GRA office, through a GRA field officer, or through your trade association.
- **Pay** with Mobile Money in the app, or by dialling **\*880#**.
- At the end of the year, report your actual sales in the app.

## VAT: who must register

From **1 January 2026** (VAT Act 2025, Act 1151):

- A business that **sells goods** must register for **VAT** once its sales pass **GHS 750,000 a year**. Most micro and small traders are below this.
- A business that **provides services** (for example printing, repairs, design, catering, consulting) must register **whatever its sales**.
- The old 3% flat rate and the COVID-19 levy have been abolished.

If you are not sure whether you must register, speak to GRA before you start charging VAT. Only a registered business may charge it.

## How VAT is worked out

Three taxes are charged together, all on the price **before tax**:

- **NHIL** (National Health Insurance Levy): 2.5%
- **GETFund** levy: 2.5%
- **VAT**: 15%

That is **20%** in total. For a job worth **GHS 2,000** before tax:

- Price before tax: GHS 2,000.00
- NHIL 2.5%: GHS 50.00
- GETFund 2.5%: GHS 50.00
- VAT 15%: GHS 300.00
- **Price with tax: GHS 2,400.00**

If you only know the price **with** tax, the price before tax is that amount × 100 ÷ 120. For GHS 2,400: 2,400 × 100 ÷ 120 = 2,000.

The tax you charge is **not your money**. It belongs to GRA. File a VAT return and pay it by the last working day of the month after the sale. The VAT you paid on your own purchases (input VAT) can reduce what you pay, if you have valid GRA VAT invoices for those purchases.

## Withholding tax (WHT)

When a business, a school or a government office pays you more than **GHS 2,000** (before VAT), it may keep back part of the payment as **withholding tax** and pay it to GRA for you:

- **Goods:** 3%
- **Works** (for example building work): 5%
- **Services:** 7.5%

Example: a printing job of **GHS 3,000** before tax is GHS 3,600 with VAT. The customer withholds 7.5% of 3,000 = **GHS 225** and pays you **GHS 3,375**. (The GHS 2,000 job above is not more than GHS 2,000, so nothing is withheld.) Always ask for the **WHT certificate**. The tax withheld is **still your income**, and each certificate counts against the income tax you owe for the year, so keep them safely.

When **you** pay a supplier more than GHS 2,000, the same rules may require you to withhold and pay it to GRA. Pay the supplier the amount after WHT, plus NHIL, GETFund and VAT only if they are VAT-registered and give you a valid GRA VAT invoice.

## Using this app for tax

- Record every sale, and your monthly sales total is on the Home screen.
- Keep receipts for expenses (you can photograph them).
- At year end, add up your monthly sales to know which MTS group you fall in.
- If you are VAT-registered, switch it on in **Settings → Registrations**. Your invoices then show NHIL, GETFund and VAT separately, everyday sales record the tax inside the price, and costs with a supplier's VAT invoice record the VAT you can claim back. **Reports → Tax** shows what you owe GRA.
- When a customer keeps back withholding tax, record it with the payment on the invoice. The invoice is settled, and the tax withheld is listed in **Reports → Tax**.
- **Reports → Tax** also has a calculator for VAT and withholding tax.
