Lenders want to know two things: *can this business repay?* and *will this owner repay?* Your job is to show both, with evidence.

## Before you borrow, ask yourself

- **What exactly is the money for?** Stock for a busy season, a machine, a second outlet? Loans for things that bring in more money are easier to repay.
- **How will it bring in more money?** Write down how many more sales you expect.
- **Can I afford the repayments in a slow month?** If the monthly repayment is more than your profit in a bad month, borrow less.

## What lenders usually ask for

Prepare these before you go:

- **Ghana Card** (and those of any partners)
- **Business registration certificate**, and permits if your trade needs them
- **Proof of sales:** several months of records, MoMo statements and bank statements
- **A short business plan:** what you sell, to whom, and how the loan will be used
- **Guarantor or collateral** details, depending on the lender

## Your records are your strongest evidence

Lenders trust numbers they can check. Six to twelve months of steady records showing sales, expenses and profit make a big difference. Keep using this app every day; your monthly totals on the Home screen are exactly what a loan officer wants to see.

Also, **receive payments into MoMo or a bank account** where you can. Statements are proof of your sales.

## Build your credit history

Credit bureaus in Ghana keep records of how people and businesses repay. Repaying small loans on time, including from microfinance companies and savings and loans companies, builds a history that helps you get bigger loans later.

## Compare before you sign

Ask every lender for:

- the **total amount** you will repay, not just the interest rate,
- **all fees** (processing, insurance, compulsory savings),
- the **repayment schedule** and what happens if you pay late.

Never sign anything you don't understand. Take it home, read it, and ask someone you trust.
